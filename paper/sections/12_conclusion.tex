\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Asking a language model what changed, in a closed vocabulary, and testing the answer on later
data is a sound interface; letting the test switch the answer on is not enough. The registered
gate was safe and nearly inert. Certify-then-hedge keeps the certificate and changes its use:
the \eprocess{} prices hypotheses, the data size them, and the controller hedges at the critical
fractile of the resulting mixture. That rule keeps the gate's error budget as a bound on expected
exposure for any model output, contains the gate as a special case, and, in a test registered
before any outcome was seen, beat both the gate and the operations research baseline with two
proposer models and four more. The same study says plainly where the value came from on this
simulator: from the certificate and the sizing, not from the language, whose measurable
contribution was to cut commitment on no-shock episodes. Whether text earns more where telemetry
is weaker, with stochastic lead times, rarer alerts or shocks that data cannot size, is the
question this design is now able to answer.
