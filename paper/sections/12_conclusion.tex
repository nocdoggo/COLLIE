\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Asking a language model what changed, in a closed vocabulary, and testing the answer on later
data is a sound interface; letting the test switch the answer on is not enough. The registered
gate was safe and nearly inert. Certify-then-hedge keeps the certificate and changes its use:
the \eprocess{} prices hypotheses, the data size them, and the controller hedges at the critical
fractile of the resulting mixture. Under a known null that rule keeps the gate's error budget as
a bound on expected exposure for any model output, and in an exploratory test registered before
its live runs it beat both the gate and the operations research baseline with two proposer models
and four more. The same study says plainly where the value came from on this simulator: from the
certificate, the sizing and the alert timing, concentrated in lead-time shifts, not from the
text, whose measured effect at the benchmark's middle cost ratio was to cut commitment on
no-shock episodes. Replays at the benchmark's other ratios suggest the text starts to earn when
stockouts dominate. Whether it does, under a null the guarantee fully covers, with stochastic
lead times and shocks that data cannot size, is the question this design can now answer.
