\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Asking a language model what changed, in a closed vocabulary, and testing the answer on later
data is a sound interface; letting the test switch the answer on is not enough. The registered
gate rarely acted on a false alarm, but it acted late, rarely and on the wrong magnitudes.
Certify-then-hedge keeps the certificate and changes its use: the \eprocess{} prices hypotheses,
the data size them, and the controller hedges at the critical fractile of the resulting mixture.
Under a known null that rule keeps the gate's error budget as a bound on expected exposure for
any model output, and in an exploratory test registered before its live runs it beat both the
gate and the operations research baseline with two proposer models and four more. The same study
says plainly where the value came from on this simulator: from mechanisms the alert-timed
content-free control shares (the certificate, the sizing and the timing), concentrated in
lead-time shifts, and not detectably from the text, whose measured effect at the benchmark's
middle cost ratio was to cut commitment on no-shock episodes. Post hoc replays at the benchmark's
other ratios suggest, with one of the two models, that the text starts to earn when stockouts
dominate. Whether it does, under a null the guarantee fully covers, with stochastic lead times
and shocks that data cannot size, is the question this design can now answer.
