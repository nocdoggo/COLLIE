\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Anytime-valid checks on language-model outputs.} POPPER runs
agent-designed falsification experiments under Type-I error
control~\cite{popper}; E-valuator stops failing agent trajectories with a
sequential test on verifier scores~\cite{e-valuator}. Conformal Selective
Acting certifies with Ville-type \eprocess{}es when a fine-tuned model may
act~\cite{csa}, and in Propose, Don't Judge a frozen anytime-valid referee
admits agent-proposed factors~\cite{propose-dont-judge}. All four, like
our registered gate, accept or refuse at an evidence threshold.
Certify-then-hedge hedges instead: the model only reweights a fixed,
action-aligned hypothesis set (and sets its hypothesis's onset window), the
prior-weighted \eprocess{}es become posterior odds, and the order is a
Bayes-mixture base-stock quantile. With one hypothesis and $\lambda = 0$ the
posterior's MAP decision is the gate's activation rule, up to one unit of
threshold; the exposure bound holds for any model output
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exposure}).

\textbf{Anytime-valid inference.} We take e-values and Ville's inequality as
given~\cite{vovk-wang-evalues, ramdas-savi, ville} and read an \eprocess{} as a
bettor's wealth~\cite{shafer-betting}. Universal inference builds a valid e-value
for a composite null by data splitting, the split likelihood-ratio
statistic~\cite{universal-inference}; we use a plug-in demand null instead, so
our guarantees hold only as far as it does
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Gr\"unwald's e-posterior has risk bounds that
loosen, not fail, when its e-collection, the analogue of a prior, is chosen
badly~\cite{grunwald-eposterior}, and e-values keep Type-I risk control when the
loss is chosen after the data~\cite{grunwald-beyond-np}. Certify-then-hedge
multiplies prior weights by \eprocess{}es where a Bayes posterior uses
likelihood ratios, so a wrong language prior costs at most
$\log(|\mathcal{H}|/\lambda)$ nats of evidence relative to trusting the true
family outright, and no validity.

\textbf{Inventory under uncertain information.} In inventory theory, advance
demand information is a reliable signal that offsets lead
time~\cite{hariharan-zipkin} and makes state-dependent base-stock policies
optimal~\cite{gallego-ozer}. With the demand law unknown, the classical answers
are Bayesian updating~\cite{scarf-bayes} and a min-max order over all laws
with given mean and variance~\cite{scarf-minmax}. Our signal is text of
unknown reliability that only splits a fixed prior budget. Unlike a Bayesian
order, the mass that moves ours comes from \eprocess{}es that stay valid
whatever working likelihood sizes the shock; unlike the min-max order, it stays
near arm~1 until evidence arrives.

\textbf{Language models in operations.} On InventoryBench, language
models augmented with operations research outperform either
alone~\cite{inventorybench}. Language models read event text into
demand forecasts~\cite{eventcast} and turn context into predicted disturbances
for a model predictive controller~\cite{instructmpc}; LLM-SAA optimizes
decisions such as the newsvendor under model-generated
distributions~\cite{llm-saa}; agents record plans as falsifiable commitments and
hypotheses with a tracked lifecycle~\cite{fcpagent, hep}. STOCKTAKE finds a
knowing-doing gap: models detect 84 to 88\% of hidden failures, yet where stress
persists 34 to 43\% of correctly diagnosed stress weeks still end in a
stockout~\cite{stocktake}. Certify-then-hedge takes no order and no demand
distribution from the model; shock sizes come from the data, and the order
moves toward a hypothesis only as its \eprocess{} grows.
