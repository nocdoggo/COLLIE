\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Why the gate was stopped}

The registered Gate~3 pilot read \emph{kill-or-reframe} on two triggers. The first was
headroom: on the registered gross-profit endpoint, even a clairvoyant that knows the shock at
onset clears the 5\% bar
on only two of six families (a demand rise and a lead-time shift), because pulses, loss bursts
and the compound family's transit pauses are over before an order placed at onset can land, and
a demand fall offers little to recover. The second compared the gate with a detector compiled
without language and found it behind by 566 per episode in gross profit; forensic replays traced
that gap to the endpoint, since on net reward the same contrast is $+2{,}306$. The exploratory
pilot then gave the gate real model content. Gemini and Grok named the right family and
direction in 59 and 53 of 96 shocked episodes, yet the gate
activated only 43\% and 39\% of the right-family proposals, a median of 10 and 11 periods after
the proposal, and ended at $-234$ and $-172$ net per episode against arm~1 with intervals
including zero. The magnitude field matched the truth's bin in 8 and 7 of 96 episodes, but
that measures a label mismatch rather than calibration: a provisional binning puts 80 of the 96
shocks in \emph{low}, while the hidden label of every non-distractor template says
\emph{medium}. Acting at once was
far worse: arm~8 lost 2{,}549 and 2{,}624 per episode.

\subsection{The fresh-seed confirmation}

Table~\ref{tab:confirm} gives the primary contrasts on the 240 fresh episodes: all four
one-sided tests reject after Holm correction (largest $p = 0.0031$), and post hoc, without the six
clusters a structural test had touched before registration, the contrasts are $+329$, $+188$,
$+163$ and $+145$ (largest $p = 0.0024$). On the 48 null episodes the method is non-inferior to
arm~1 at the registered margin of $-25$ (Gemini $-3.2$ $[-8.2, -0.1]$, Grok $+1.9$
$[-1.7, 8.2]$); post hoc, Gemini's small loss comes from the false-alert twins. The gate is no
better than arm~1 on fresh seeds, and with four further proposer models
(Fig.~\ref{fig:models}) every interval for the method against the gate and arm~1 excludes zero.

\subsection{Where the gain comes from}

By the registered per-family readout, the 32 shocked lead-time episodes supply 96\% of the gain
over arm~1 ($+1{,}286$ and $+1{,}177$ per episode, against $+80$ to $+95$ per demand fall and
pulse, and about $-40$ and $-3$ per compound and loss episode). The rest is post hoc: every shocked family's
interval excludes zero except the demand rise ($-83$; $[-200, 21]$ and $[-204, 26]$). Without the
lead-time units the method is $+9$ $[-24, 43]$ and $+7$ $[-25, 36]$ against arm~1; against the
gate it still leads by $+227$ $[82, 408]$ with Gemini, whose gate loses $1{,}057$ per demand-rise
episode by compiling the stated magnitude, while with Grok $+58$ $[-1, 134]$ is not
distinguishable from zero. Where the model named the shift (17 of the 32 lead-time episodes with
Gemini, 11 with Grok; in the rest the gate never acted), two mechanisms separate hedge from gate:
timing, as the hedge
commits partially as evidence accumulates while the gate commits all at once a median of 11
periods after the proposal; and sizing, as the gate compiles the stated size, wrong in 7 and 6
of those proposals (true shifts were one or two periods), while
the hedge estimates the offset from receipts (Fig.~\ref{fig:episode}).

\subsection{What the language contributes}

The content-free variant calls no model but is timed by the same alerts; it earns $+169$
$[48, 312]$ against arm~1, and at $p/h = 4$ the method is not distinguishable from it ($+9$
$[-5, 24]$ with Gemini, $-6$ $[-29, 11]$ with Grok; $-43$ to $0$ with the four further models,
below zero for Llama~3.1~8B and GPT-3.5 Turbo). What the model's label changes is commitment on
null episodes: the summed shock mass falls from $0.83$ to $0.52$ and $0.46$ (post hoc, on
false-alert twins, with no detectable change in null reward), since naming any family moves
prior weight off the others. Trusting the family alone
($\lambda = 0$) cuts it to $0.18$ and $0.06$ but costs net reward against the content-free arm
with four of the six models, from $-83$ $[-169, -13]$ (Grok) to $-192$ (Llama~3.1~8B); hence
the hedged prior. Post hoc,
with each model's answers held fixed, the weight matters less than the rule: $\lambda = 0.5$ or
$0.75$ is not distinguishable from $0.25$, while committing once the shock mass reaches $1/2$
changes net reward by $-56$ $[-97, -19]$ and $-62$ $[-108, -21]$ ($-370$ and $-368$ at
$p/h = 19$).

\subsection{Cost ratios and noisy lead times}
\label{sec:stage-d}

A second registered stage, exploratory with respect to Gate~3, ran the frozen method on a new
pool of 48 units at the benchmark's other ratios, $p/h = 19$ and $1$, and at $p/h = 4$ on eight
lead-time units whose lead times carry the registered arrival law's noise (clipped at four
periods), with Holm's correction within each family of tests. At $p/h = 19$ the method beat the
gate by $+597$ $[323, 917]$ (Gemini) and $+631$ $[265, 1{,}085]$ (Grok) and arm~1 by $+979$
$[364, 1{,}703]$ and $+896$ $[322, 1{,}569]$, and against the content-free variant the text
earned $+82$ $[35, 138]$ with Gemini ($+84$ $[18, 166]$ in a post-hoc replay of the first
stage's episodes) but $-2$ $[-75, 59]$ with Grok; five of the six tests reject. On null episodes
the difference from arm~1 is $-22$ $[-65, 5]$ with each model, so non-inferiority at $-25$ is not
established. At $p/h = 1$ the method beat the gate by $+189$ $[103, 288]$ and $+45$ $[10, 86]$
(both reject; the gate itself loses to arm~1 there), is not distinguishable from arm~1 ($+10$
$[-24.5, 51.8]$, just non-inferior at $-25$; $+4$ $[-27.6, 43.3]$, not non-inferior) or,
two-sided, from the content-free variant, and loses a little on null episodes ($-3.5$
$[-7.0, -0.8]$, $-3.3$ $[-6.7, -0.7]$). Per unit of price the gain over arm~1 rises from
$p/h = 1$ to $19$ with Grok ($+43$ $[2, 80]$; Gemini $+41$ $[-1, 79]$). On noisy lead times the
gain over arm~1, $+254$ and $+187$, was not confirmed (exact $p = 0.14$ and $0.15$; six of eight
unit gains positive with each model; descriptive intervals $[-141, 629]$ and $[-149, 469]$); it
came from the four units whose lead rose by two periods, while the one-period units lost on
average (shift size is confounded with the baseline lead).

\subsection{Model scale}

The ladder ran 20 of the 21 models on the 120 pilot episodes (StepFun's slowest reasoning model
had not finished at the time of writing). First proposals named the right family and direction
in 0 to 59 of 96 shocked episodes. Acting at once lost between nothing (Llama~3.2 1B, which never
produced a usable \shockspec{}) and $3{,}656$ per episode (Gemini~2.5 Flash-Lite). Contrary to the stage's
registered expectation that this loss would grow as perception falls, it grows with how readily
a model commits, and commitment and accuracy move together (post hoc, $r = 0.86$). The gate
stayed between $-300$ and $+3$ against arm~1 and acted in at most one of the 12 false-alert twin
episodes; contrary to the same expectation, its interval lay below zero on two models
(Gemini~2.5 Flash $-294$ $[-567, -45]$, GPT-3.5 Turbo $-60$ $[-151, -1]$). The hedge's
development replays (no claim) had point estimates between $+107$ and $+158$ on every model,
against $+136$ for its content-free variant.
