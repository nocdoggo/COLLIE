\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Why the gate was stopped}

The registered Gate~3 pilot read \emph{kill-or-reframe} on two triggers. The first was
headroom: even a clairvoyant that knows the shock at onset clears the registered 5\% profit bar
on only two of six families (a demand rise and a lead-time shift), because pulses, loss bursts
and pauses are over before an order placed at onset can land. The second compared the gate with
a detector compiled without language and found it behind by 566 per episode in gross profit;
forensic replays traced that gap to the endpoint, since on net reward the same contrast is
$+2{,}306$. The exploratory pilot then gave the gate real model content. Gemini and Grok named
the right family and direction in 59 and 53 of 96 shocked episodes (a scripted fixed answer
manages 16), yet the gate activated only 43\% and 39\% of the right-family proposals, a median of
10 and 11 periods after the proposal, and ended at $-234$ and $-172$ net per episode against
arm~1 with intervals including zero. The magnitude field was right in 8 and 7 of 96 episodes,
partly because the alert templates themselves label every non-distractor shock
\emph{medium}. Acting at once was far worse: arm~8 lost 2{,}549 and 2{,}624 per episode.

\subsection{The registered confirmation}

Table~\ref{tab:confirm} gives the primary contrasts on the 240 fresh episodes. Certify-then-hedge
beats the gate by $309$ (Gemini) and $179$ (Grok) net per episode and arm~1 by $179$ and $163$;
all four one-sided tests reject after Holm correction (largest adjusted threshold $0.05$,
largest $p = 0.0031$). Gross profit agrees on both contrasts. On the 48 null episodes the method
is non-inferior to arm~1 with both models (Gemini $-3.2$ $[-8.2, -0.1]$, Grok $+1.9$
$[-1.7, 8.2]$, margin $-25$). The gate itself is no better than arm~1 on fresh seeds
($-131$ and $-16$ net, intervals including zero), and acting at once loses $2{,}046$ and
$2{,}284$. Fig.~\ref{fig:models} repeats the confirmation with four further proposer models;
every interval for the method against the gate and against arm~1 excludes zero.

\subsection{Where the gain comes from}

The gain is where the headroom is. Against arm~1, certify-then-hedge earns $+1{,}286$ (Gemini)
and $+1{,}177$ (Grok) per lead-time episode, $+90$ and $+88$ per demand-fall episode and $+95$
and $+80$ per pulse episode; it gives back $83$ per demand-rise episode, about $40$ per compound
episode and about $3$ per loss episode, and $3$ or less per null. Two mechanisms carry the
lead-time gain. The first is sizing: the model called
most one-period lead-time shifts \emph{medium}, which the gate compiles as two periods, while
the within-hypothesis posterior recovered the true offset from receipts (Fig.~\ref{fig:episode}).
The second is timing: the hedge commits partially as soon as the evidence starts to accumulate
rather than all at once ten periods later. Both are statistical, which the next result makes
explicit.

\subsection{What the language contributes}

The content-free variant, which never calls a model, earns $+169$ $[48, 312]$ against arm~1, and
the method is within $\pm 10$ of it on net with both primary models ($+9$ and $-6$; with the four
further models, $-43$ to $+1$). On this simulator, then, a well-certified hypothesis search
recovers the available headroom without language. What the model's family label does change is
commitment on no-shock episodes: the summed shock mass on null episodes falls from $0.83$
(content-free) to $0.46$ to $0.52$ with the hedged prior, about 45\% less. Trusting the model's
family alone ($\lambda = 0$) cuts that further, to $0.04$ to $0.18$, but costs net reward
($-16$ and $-83$ against content-free with Gemini and Grok, $-192$ with Llama~3.1~8B), which is
why the prior is hedged. Section~\ref{sec:hedge}'s bound makes the trade explicit: a wrong family
costs at most $\log(|\mathcal{H}|/\lambda) = \log 16$ nats of evidence.

\subsection{Model scale}

The ladder ran 18 of the 21 models on the 120 pilot episodes (the three slowest reasoning models
had not finished at the time of writing). First proposals named the right family and direction
in 0 to 59 of 96 shocked episodes: Llama~3.2 1B and 3B never produced a valid \shockspec{}, so
every proposal fell back to the baseline, and Qwen~2.5 7B abstained or failed on almost every
call. Acting on proposals at once lost between nothing (the models that never commit) and
$3{,}656$ net per episode (Gemini~2.5 Flash-Lite, which almost never abstains); how much a model
loses tracks how readily it commits, not how often it is right. The gate stayed between $-300$
and $+3$ against arm~1 on every model, and acted on a false alert in at most one of 12 null
episodes. Certify-then-hedge, replayed on each model's cached answers during development (so
without a claim), gained between $+107$ and $+158$ on every model, including the ones that
produced nothing usable, because an absent or wrong family only removes the model's share of
the prior.
