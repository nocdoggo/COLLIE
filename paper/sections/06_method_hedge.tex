\section{Certify, Then Hedge}
\label{sec:hedge}

The registered gate of Section~\ref{sec:verify} answers one question, whether a
hypothesis may act, and answers it late: it waits until $E_{j,t}$ crosses
$1/\alpha_j$ and then acts on the magnitude the model stated. With live models
(Section~\ref{sec:results}) both halves of that design failed in the same place: the
gate refuted none of the right-family proposals (0 of 67 and 0 of 74), but it
activated less than half of them, a median of 10 to 11 periods after the proposal,
and when it did act it compiled whatever magnitude the model had stated, though no
alert states a size.
Certify-then-hedge keeps the \eprocess{} and changes what it is used for. The
\eprocess{} no longer switches a controller on; it prices the hypothesis, and the
controller hedges between the baseline and the hypothesis according to that
price. Fig.~\ref{fig:method} follows one firing through the construction.

\subsection{Hypotheses and the language prior}

At each trigger firing $j$ (a stopping time $\tau_j$, at most two per episode) we
register a fixed, action-aligned hypothesis set $\mathcal{H}$: demand up, demand
down, lead-time shift and an upward demand pulse. The other families (\texttt{shipment\_loss},
\texttt{transit\_pause}, \texttt{compound}) carry no hypothesis by design: an order
placed at onset cannot land before a loss burst or a pause ends, and in the pilot's
forensic replay even a clairvoyant acting at onset gained under 1\% on loss bursts or
compound shocks once its foresight of demand noise was removed. Hypothesis $k \in \mathcal{H}$ of firing
$j$ receives prior mass $a_j w_{jk}$ with
\begin{equation}
a_j = \alpha\,2^{-j}, \qquad
w_{jk} = \frac{\lambda}{|\mathcal{H}|} + (1-\lambda)\,\Ind\{k = \hat k_j\},
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
where $\hat k_j$ is the hypothesis the language model's \shockspec{} names (none if
it abstains, fails to parse after one repair, or names a family and direction
outside $\mathcal{H}$) and $\lambda\in[0,1]$ is a registered hedge. The model
therefore sets the prior weights and, for the hypothesis it names, the onset window
of its \eprocess{}; nothing else. With $\lambda = 1$ the arm never calls the model
(it is still timed by the shared trigger, alerts included), and $\lambda = 0$
trusts the model's family alone. The allocation $a_j$ is the frozen budget of
Theorem~\ref{thm:activation}, spent on masses rather than thresholds; over both
firings it totals $A = \sum_j a_j \le 3\alpha/4$.

\subsection{Certification, posterior and sizing}

Each $(j,k)$ carries its own \eprocess{} $E_{jk,t}$ of the form
\eqref{eq:eprocess}, started strictly after $\tau_j$ and built from a canonical
specification of $k$ (with the model's onset window when $k = \hat k_j$). Every
hypothesis keeps receiving evidence after a later firing, so nothing is lost to
supersession. With $A_t = \sum_{j:\tau_j < t}\sum_k a_j w_{jk}$ and
$N_t = \sum_{j,k} a_j w_{jk} E_{jk,t}$, the predictable posterior mass of $(j,k)$
at decision $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\pi_{jk,t} = \frac{a_j w_{jk}\, E_{jk,t-1}}{1 - A_t + N_{t-1}},
\qquad
\Pi_t = \sum_{j,k} \pi_{jk,t}.
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
Validity of what follows needs nothing from the \emph{size} of a shock, so the
size comes from the data rather than the model's magnitude bin. Within a demand
hypothesis a working likelihood of the visible demand path weighs the registered
multipliers over a grid of starts and durations, and the post-change level is
learned conjugately (five pseudo-observations at the hypothesized multiple of the
pre-onset mean); within the lead-time hypothesis a Gaussian working likelihood of
visible receipts and the in-transit total weighs offsets $\Delta \in \{1,2,3\}$
and starts. Each $(j,k)$ thus contributes a predictive law $G_{jk,t}$ for demand
over the protection interval, and the null contributes arm~1's own
$F_{0,t} = \mathcal{N}\bigl((1+L)\bar d_t,\,(1+L)s_{d,t}^2\bigr)$.

\subsection{The hedge}

The order-up-to requirement is the critical fractile $\beta = p/(p+h)$ of the
posterior-predictive mixture of the requirement $Y - P_t$, where $P_t$ is the
in-transit total,
\begin{multline}
x_t = \inf\Bigl\{x : (1-\Pi_t)\,F_{0,t}(x + P_t) \\
+ \sum_{j,k}\pi_{jk,t}\,G_{jk,t}(x + P_t) \ge \beta\Bigr\},
\label{eq:hedge}
\end{multline}
and the order is $q_t = \min(\max(0,\lceil x_t - I_t\rceil), C_t, \hat C_t)$ with
the published smoother $\hat C_t$ unchanged. When no hypothesis is live,
$\Pi_t = 0$ and $q_t$ is arm~1's order bit for bit.

\subsection{Guarantees}

Write $\Prob_0$ for the global no-change null under which every $E_{jk}$ is a
nonnegative supermartingale with unit start (the model-conditional premise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:activation}); this needs the null's demand baseline to be known
and the delays to follow the registered arrival law. A hypothesis whose
\eprocess{} cannot be built, or which meets an observation impossible under its
law, is dropped from both $N$ and $A_t$, which only lowers $\Pi_t$; one whose
window has closed is kept.

\begin{theorem}[Exposure]
\label{thm:exposure}
For every stopping time $\sigma$ of the decision filtration $(\Filt_{t-1})$ and
every language-model output, including abstention, a wrong family or adversarial
content, $\Expect_0[\Pi_\sigma] \le A$ and, for $c\in(0,1)$,
$\Prob_0(\sup_t \Pi_t \ge c) \le A(1-c)/\bigl(c(1-A)\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch.} Add the budget of firings yet to come,
$\tilde N_t = N_t + \sum_{j:\tau_j > t} a_j$. Because the weights are fixed at
each $\tau_j$ and sum to at most one, $\tilde N$ is a nonnegative supermartingale
with $\tilde N_0 \le A$. Since $A_t \le A$ and $N \le \tilde N$,
$\Pi_t \le \tilde N_{t-1}/(1-A+\tilde N_{t-1})$, which is concave and increasing, so
optional stopping at $\sigma-1$ and Jensen's inequality give the first bound, and
Ville's inequality applied to $\tilde N/A$ gives the second. \hfill$\square$

\begin{theorem}[Odds regret]
\label{thm:regret}
Let $c_F(x)$ be the one-period newsvendor cost of target $x$ under law $F$, $x^0_t$
arm~1's target, $G_t = \Pi_t^{-1}\sum_{j,k}\pi_{jk,t}G_{jk,t}$ the hypotheses'
mixture and $V_t = c_{G_t}(x^0_t) - \min_x c_{G_t}(x) \le \bar V$ the value of acting
on them if they were true. Then
$c_{F_0}(x_t) - c_{F_0}(x^0_t) \le \frac{\Pi_t}{1-\Pi_t}\,V_t$, so under
$\Prob_0$ the expected cumulative surrogate regret is at most
$T A \bar V/(1-A)$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch.} $x_t$ minimizes $(1-\Pi_t)c_{F_0} + \Pi_t c_{G_t}$, so
$(1-\Pi_t)\bigl(c_{F_0}(x_t)-c_{F_0}(x^0_t)\bigr) \le
\Pi_t\bigl(c_{G_t}(x^0_t)-c_{G_t}(x_t)\bigr) \le \Pi_t V_t$; and
$\Pi_t/(1-\Pi_t) = N_{t-1}/(1-A_t) \le \tilde N_{t-1}/(1-A)$ with
$\Expect_0[\tilde N_{t-1}] \le A$. \hfill$\square$

The premise is fragile as run: the demand \eprocess{}es estimate the null's mean and spread
from the periods before $\tau_j$, and outside the noisy-lead stratum the simulator's delays are
deterministic, not the registered arrival law; a null simulation puts
$\Expect_0[\Pi_\sigma]$ at up to 2.7 times $A$ in the cells matched to our null episodes
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Two further properties follow from \eqref{eq:prior} and \eqref{eq:posterior}.
First, with one hypothesis and $\lambda = 0$ the posterior odds are
$a_j E_{j,t}/(1-a_j)$, so thresholding $E_{j,t}$ at $1/\alpha_j$, as the gate
does, is up to one unit of threshold acting when that hypothesis is the more
probable. This is an analogy, not an identity. The evidence is largely shared: for a
named demand or pulse hypothesis the gate's \eprocess{} mixes over the registered
multipliers whatever magnitude the model states, and for a lead-time shift the
magnitude only selects the delay tilt. What differs is its use: the gate switches all
at once to the configuration compiled from the stated magnitude, while the hedge sizes
the shock from the data and weights it by its posterior mass. Second, the language model can move evidence but not
validity. A hypothesis the model does not name enters with $\lambda$ times its
content-free weight, so a wrong or missing family costs exactly
$\log(1/\lambda)$ nats of evidence against the arm with $\lambda = 1$ ($\log 4$
at $\lambda = 1/4$); the named hypothesis enters with
$\lambda + (1-\lambda)|\mathcal{H}|$ times it, a saving of
$\log(\lambda + (1-\lambda)|\mathcal{H}|)$ ($\log 3.25$). Neither changes the
bounds above. Theorem~\ref{thm:regret} is stated for the one-period surrogate that
the controller optimizes; lost sales, rounding, the caps and the zero floor make
realized multi-period cost an empirical matter, which Section~\ref{sec:results}
measures.
