\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{One simulator, 48 units.} All evidence comes from our generator. Its
lead times are deterministic, so a lead-time shift appears in the receipts
without noise, and at $p/h = 4$ that is where the gain sits: lead-time shifts, a
sixth of the shocked episodes, carry about 171 of the 178 per-episode net gain
over arm~1 with Gemini and 157 of 163 with Grok, while the point estimates are
$-83$ per demand-rise and $-40$ per compound episode (the registered readout
gives no per-family intervals). The method has not been run on InventoryBench's
own instances, a third of which have stochastic lead times with lost shipments;
the fresh-seed test used one of the benchmark's three cost ratios (the others
are replayed post hoc with the models' answers held fixed), and 48 units rather
than the registered 72, because the alert bank yields distinct texts for only
eight units per family.

\textbf{What the theorems cover.} Theorems~\ref{thm:exposure}
and~\ref{thm:regret} assume an exact null, and the arm as run lies outside it:
its demand null is estimated from the pre-proposal prefix, and its arrival null
is a diffuse delay law while our delays are deterministic. A null Monte Carlo of
the frozen arm (fixed false payload, scripted firings; released with our code)
finds no cell more than two standard errors above a bound where the premise
holds. With the plug-in null and a first firing at period 10, in the cells
matched to our null twins (demand spread 25\%, deterministic delays, a demand-up or
content-free prior), it measures $\Expect_0[\Pi_\sigma]$ at 2.0 to 2.7 times $A$ and
$\Prob_0(\sup_t \Pi_t \ge 1/2)$ at 2.3 to 3.7 times its bound, and up to 3.2 and
4.7 times in other cells; later firings shrink the excess without removing it. The arrival mismatch alone keeps stopped
and summed exposure below their bounds but lifts exposure at a fixed decision
to at most 1.24 times its budget. The generator's exact demand law also
scores integer demands on a rounding lattice, which may certify faster than
unrounded data would. The regret theorem bounds a one-period newsvendor
surrogate only.

\textbf{What the content-free control measures.} The control calls no model but
is timed by the same alerts, so it removes the text, not the alert channel. Its
null commitment also overstates action: a pulse hypothesis keeps its \eprocess{}
after its window closes, so its mass can linger on a null episode after it stops
moving the order. Post hoc, crediting each period's mass to its leading hypothesis,
pulse-led periods carry 0.69 of the control's 0.83 and 0.32 and 0.38 of the hedge's 0.52
and 0.46, so most of what the model's label removes is that mass.

\textbf{Status of the evidence.} Relative to Gate~3 this study is exploratory,
and Gate~3's registered decision on the pilot, \emph{kill-or-reframe}, stands.
The method was developed on the 120 spent pilot episodes; its fresh-seed
confirmation was registered, with the code frozen by hash, before its live runs.
The plan discloses a structural test on six of the 48 units beforehand, an
interim look at both primary tests on one model's run, three departures from the
original registration (net reward as the primary endpoint, action before any
activation threshold, fresh units outside the frozen manifest) and three
amendments, the last recording an independent audit that reproduced every
registered number and listed further departures, none of which changed a run, a
rule or a number.
