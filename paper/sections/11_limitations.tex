\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{One simulator, one cost ratio, 48 units.} All evidence comes from our
generator. Its lead times are deterministic, so a lead-time shift appears in the
receipts without noise, and that is where the gain sits: lead-time shifts, a
sixth of the shocked episodes, carry about 171 of the 178 per-episode net gain
over arm~1 with Gemini and 157 of 163 with Grok, while the point estimates are
$-83$ per demand-rise and $-40$ per compound episode (the registered readout
gives no per-family intervals). The method has not been run on InventoryBench's
own instances, a third of which have stochastic lead times with lost shipments,
across three cost ratios; the fresh-seed test used one, $p/h = 4$, and 48 units
rather than the registered 72, because the alert bank yields distinct texts for
only eight units per family.

\textbf{What the theorems cover.} Theorems~\ref{thm:exposure}
and~\ref{thm:regret} assume an exact null, and the arm as run lies outside it:
its demand null is estimated from the pre-proposal prefix, and its arrival null
is a diffuse delay law while our delays are deterministic. A null Monte Carlo of
the frozen arm (fixed false payload, scripted firings; released with our code)
finds no cell more than two standard errors above a bound where the premise
holds. With the plug-in null and a first firing at period 10 it measures
$\Expect_0[\Pi_\sigma]$ at 2.0 to 2.7 times $A$ and
$\Prob_0(\sup_t \Pi_t \ge 1/2)$ at 2.3 to 3.7 times its bound; later firings
shrink the excess without removing it. The arrival mismatch alone keeps stopped
and summed exposure below their bounds but lifts exposure at a fixed decision
to at most 1.24 times its budget. The generator's exact demand law also
scores integer demands on a rounding lattice, which certifies faster than
unrounded data would. The regret theorem bounds a one-period newsvendor
surrogate only.

\textbf{What the content-free control measures.} The control with
$\lambda = 1$ calls no model but is timed by the same alerts, so it removes the
text, not the alert channel. On fresh seeds the method's net reward is not
distinguishable from the control's: $+9$ $[-5, 24]$ per episode with Gemini and
$-6$ $[-29, 11]$ with Grok, and with Llama~3.1~8B ($-43$ $[-78, -15]$) and
GPT-3.5 Turbo ($-16$ $[-35, -0.4]$) the control earns more. The family label
mainly lowers the summed shock mass on null episodes, from 0.83 to 0.52
(Gemini) and 0.46 (Grok), mostly on false-alert nulls, by moving prior weight
off the pulse hypothesis. That measure overstates action: a pulse hypothesis
keeps its \eprocess{} after its window closes, so its mass can linger on a null
episode after it stops moving the order.

\textbf{Status of the evidence.} Relative to Gate~3 this study is exploratory,
and Gate~3's registered decision on the pilot, \emph{kill-or-reframe}, stands.
The method was developed on the 120 spent pilot episodes. Its fresh-seed
confirmation was registered, with the code frozen by hash, before the
confirmation runs; the plan discloses a structural test on six of the 48 units
beforehand, an interim look at one primary endpoint, three departures from the
original registration (net reward as the primary endpoint, action before any
activation threshold, fresh units outside the frozen manifest) and two
amendments.
