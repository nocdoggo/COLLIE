# Outcome-free follow-up components

These components are development candidates. They are not a pushed amendment, an authorization to spend, or a sealed-data reader. No real follow-up sample, response, reference judgment, or outcome is included here. No new human annotation is planned. Existing human annotations and task E remain unchanged; machine-generated reference judgments are not human gold.

## Components

- `records.py`, `parsing.py`, `references.py`, and `semantic.py` validate typed readings, preserve independent judgments and alternatives, enforce the ordered N missingness rules, and distinguish returned failures from unavailable responses. A unique end remains usable when another reference field is unresolved. Its overall reading class can remain unknown.
- `machine_references.py` separately records blinded M1/M2/MADJ machine judgments for N, C and four-variant R materials. The selected synthetic candidate includes blind-first MADJ and separate C absent/specified adjudications, retains disagreement and missingness, and labels its outputs as machine-generated. Generic machine validity is not generator-bound R eligibility: the latter checks the draft generator's exact date-role labels but remains disabled, and literal date quotations do not prove their semantic roles. It constructs in-memory views only and disables declared real scope. Registration, blinding and chronology fields are attestations, not independent proof; R eligibility, batching, privacy, analysis integration and actual verification remain unresolved.
- `measurement_sensitivity.py` computes outcome-free two-horizon Brier perturbations. The original private version is the starting point. This version permits an unclassified reading to have a known nonmaterial end when other reference fields are unresolved. This preserves the fixed-denominator estimand without calling an uncertain reading correct.
- `natural_analysis.py` keeps the assigned episode-balanced denominator, reports completion ranges separately from bootstrap intervals, and supplies the full displacement-by-sensitivity grid. The conditional rate has an explicit denominator. If bootstrap replicates have no errors, they are reported as undefined rather than dropped silently.
- `sampling.py` projects an explicit allowlist from hash-checked open-event bytes. It implements the historical first-sight display rule, deterministic episode and notice draws, fixed C strata, exclusion inventories, quota redistribution, and shortfall accounting. A draw is refused until each exclusion category is explicitly marked complete. The C draw is tied to the matching N frame and excludes matching N wording as well as IDs. Its expression rules and salts remain draft decisions until recorded before collection. `exclusions.py` prepares identities for the registered sample lists, task E, original guide examples, quoted phrases, and prompt canary. It does not establish that other item-level exposure or later prompt development is absent.
- `catalog_gates.py` is a separate candidate for binding six exclusion categories to source hashes, explicit evidence and attestation identities, an independently recorded regeneration and hash-checked event bytes. Versioned fingerprints, keyed aliases and an N/C protocol lock are tested on invented data; the latest closure-binding revision needs separate review. Public direct draws are disabled, but private Python capabilities are not a sandbox. Legacy inventories must be regenerated, not relabelled; attestations and commitments do not prove coverage, entropy or registration. Keep manifests private: deterministic ranks and counts can still reveal identities. No actual N/C frame or sample may be drawn until all exposure, input-authorization and protocol gates are resolved.
- `role_materials.py` defines legacy fictional-generator mechanics, a fixed reserve, SHA256-selected primary orientations, an excluded practice set and earlier human-validation interfaces. Its public evaluation generator is disabled; private functions are not an access-control boundary. A legacy deterministic evaluation candidate was instantiated once in a local guard test and must not be treated as fresh R80 material. Synthetic checks use a separate, permanently excluded namespace. `role_analysis.py` implements paired calculations but its human-validation interface cannot establish machine-reference provenance; a separate machine adapter is required. `role_power.py` supplies planning proxies only.
- `role_generator_v2.py` is a separate synthetic-only candidate for private seed commitments and domain-separated HMAC aliases, addressing predictability of the original draft's role IDs. Its latest repr and Unicode checks pass 97 invented-data tests and await separate re-review. The lock fence lasts only for one loaded module instance; real/evaluation scope, reserve replacement and machine-reference integration remain disabled or unresolved. A code check cannot prove secret entropy, secrecy or a pushed registration.
- `role_pilot_v2.py` is a separate synthetic-only three-seed/12-variant pilot-lineage candidate. Its fixed target templates are textually distinct from the declared evaluation families, but near-duplicate separation, secure material provenance, real pilot use and machine-reference integration remain unresolved. Its in-process no-feedback assertion is not proof of timing or registration.
- `annotation_packs.py` retains legacy in-memory pack mechanics for invented-data tests only. It does not enforce the newer evidence gate, uses raw N/C IDs and predictable role aliases, and is not an approved real-pack interface. No new human judgments are planned, and the completed annotations remain untouched.
- `prompts.py` holds draft N, C, and R templates and schemas. The convention block corrects the `within N` distinction and explicitly records guide and rule-reader differences in the private review. A small neutral-draft revision meets the 5% block-length target under a public DeepSeek-V3 tokenizer candidate, but that asset is not verified against the actual provider and the official Llama tokenizer is gated. Both actual tokenizers and semantic neutrality remain unverified; do not lock or call the prompts yet.
- `execution.py` builds route-pinned requests for the two tested-model lanes and a persistent SQLite accounting ledger. It accepts only a scripted, non-network transport and cannot execute the reference-model lanes. The mock requires an explicit shared retry quota and a separately supplied per-attempt charge ceiling covering billable output beyond the requested cap; neither supplied evidence nor a SHA256 proves a real provider guarantee. Its latest candidate uses a shared one-sent-attempt fence, quarantines anomalous claims, rejects older schemas, and flags unverified attempt counts; recent corrections need separate review. The legacy analysis identity bridge is disabled. The ledger does not integrate historical liabilities, original per-model caps or the shared study cap. It pins `data_collection: deny` and a conservative `max_price` rate filter in the mock request, but neither a routing filter nor an at-sign check is a privacy or total-spending guarantee. Live transport, all-lane accounting, verified checkpoint and charge bounds, final pricing/tokenizers and a pushed registration remain required.
- `workload.py` counts tested and machine-reference requests, including the optional blind MADJ stage, both C reference passes, all four R variants, optional reference-only review of the fixed R reserve, excluded pilots, non-inference administrative attempts and a shared retry allowance. Its conditional price and attempt arithmetic neither verifies batching and provider limits nor implements a retry quota or replacement rule; an unbounded administrative cost remains unknown.
- `shared_budget.py` models original-model, study and programme caps together only for explicitly synthetic SQLite snapshots. It has no provider transport or integration with the original runner, and cannot authenticate billing or reserve every planned first-attempt slot. Conflicting or unrecognized claims block new synthetic reservations; the latest revision still needs independent review and cannot authorize real use.
- `oss_safeguards.py` is a separate in-memory, synthetic-only exercise for the incomplete original benchmark. It models individual nested attempts, an independently supplied charge bound, reconciliation of unknown charges, model/study caps, and immutable synthetic output merging. It cannot read real inputs, use credentials, make requests, or resume the benchmark; its simulated assurances are not evidence of real provider guarantees or a registration.
- `oss_sidecar_synthetic.py` tests a separate, non-overwriting sidecar for explicitly invented checkpoint fixtures under a restricted `/tmp/opencode` directory. An optional mock-only guard requires a settled synthetic answer ticket and no outstanding mock reservations, but the mock reply contains no actual response bytes and the guard has no durable replay protection. It rechecks invented original and sidecar bytes; an interrupted publication can leave an indeterminate artifact. It has no real-data, credential, cache or network interface and is not an approved live restart or proof of real filesystem provenance.
- `oss_replay_synthetic.py` is a separate, one-answer outbox experiment for closed synthetic replies and a single private scratch SQLite store. It commits each toy reservation and sent state before consuming a mock, retains unresolved or conflicting charges, and records a publication intent before using the invented sidecar. Tests include real process exits and concurrent fixture connections. A separate or rolled-back store, externally modified SQL, mount aliases, provider billing and actual crash durability are not authenticated or fenced. It uses toy accounting units and is not integrated with the shared budget or the original runner.
- `oss_jsonl_synthetic.py` models an in-memory literal-only subset of the original reader's two JSONL shapes. It preserves the supplied original bytes, checks item/sample/attempt linkage, and leaves orphaned response attempts explicit or links them to a later invented reading without duplicating the response. Its inputs, inventory, hashes, responses, and cache-hit flags are synthetic caller assertions; it performs no filesystem I/O, does not cover the full real schema, and is not integrated with the outbox or real checkpoints.
- `oss_checkpoint_backup.py` is a separate, copy-only utility for the 16 identified partial original runs. Its real entry point requires a pushed amendment naming the exact source/test hashes and an independently recorded remote verification; it makes a new private snapshot, never modifies the original files, and does not read caches, credentials or sealed outcomes. It cannot fence direct workers, authenticate provider billing, prove filesystem provenance or resume the run. An interrupted or failed copy may leave an incomplete snapshot for manual review.
- `oss_one_shot_transport.py` wraps one HTTPX transport so that at most one physical request is ever sent for a registered continuation: it validates the exact original model/route/decoding JSON and research user agent, refuses message or header content containing `@`, durably journals a non-content intent before dispatch, and never retries after failure, redirection or process exit. Its journal is an exact private append-only reservation; a second open descriptor or a reused ticket is refused. The $0.10 hold is not a proven charge bound and provider-side retries are not controlled by this wrapper.
- `oss_one_shot_cache.py` is a no-overwrite view over the fixed original cache root (or an invented fixture root). Existing entries are opened read-only without following the final link; new entries are published through a synced exclusive temporary and a non-overwriting hard link. Unresolved temporaries, unsafe links and conflicting entries are refused rather than cleaned up. A hit does not establish a completed reading or repair.
- `oss_one_shot_resume.py` is the prospective single-attempt continuation candidate. Its real entry refuses to run unless pushed A4 names the exact source/test/selection hashes, the commit is remotely verified, the F1-frozen sources are unchanged, the A3 snapshot matches, the local ledger fits the A2 caps and the stricter $200 reader check, a fresh key status is ≤300 seconds old, and no credential/proxy environment override is present. It skips completed and orphaned pairs, writes only a private sidecar, and appends nothing to original readings/responses/manifests. Tests cover the registration gate, context selection, budget check, installed hooks and sidecar validation with invented fixtures only.
- `cache_inventory.py` is a synthetic-only, metadata-only candidate for checking explicitly supplied content-addressed cache keys without opening response files or searching directories. It rejects real-cache paths and requires independent review before any broader use. A present file is not proof of a valid response, a completed repair, provider identity or benchmark completion.
- `calibration.py` uses the original slip-calibrator interface with fixed horizons and cells and implements the constructed-shift grid and omitted-field control. Synthetic revisions bind a supported copied state and exact comparison inputs, while F1 verification and verified forecasts fail closed because actual input and fit provenance are not established. The real fit and baseline reproduction have not been performed. Recomputed full precision and six-decimal compatibility do not prove equality to unavailable historical full-precision predictions.
- `e7.py` implements the four F1.11 reading classes and matched, outcome-free swaps against the frozen rule reference. It consumes hash-verified task E confirmed-list bytes supplied by its caller. It does not calculate realized losses or open sealed data.
- `ranking.py` implements synthetic rowwise and explicitly shared-event feasible intervals, with the original sign convention. Its scoped adapter permits only the three original comparison types per primary and labels the result exploratory. An authorized outcome reader and joint disclosure protection are still needed before real use.
- `rendering.py` renders selected hash-verified aggregate blocks without returning per-item records. It retains the six original tests, score-specific H2 equivalence, the failed registered pattern, E5 directions, and the probe's identification caveat. It flags display rounding that cannot be resolved from retained precision. Rendered results remain private until release is approved.

## Before evaluation

Complete and verify all exclusion inventories, then freeze sample frames and identities before inspecting labels or item-level outputs. Specify the machine-reference judges, blinding, prompts, batching, adjudication, pilot, fixed package and analyses before collecting evaluation responses; machine judgments must retain their provenance and uncertainty. Verify prompt lengths with both primary tokenizers and document the neutral-block review. Verify the original baseline fit and live provider identities. Name and hash any later sealed reader in a dated amendment pushed before its first read. Record any decisions made under the owner's time-limited delegation and retain all independent safety and release gates.

The private scorer candidate is separate from the F1-frozen sources. Its conservative release gate currently refuses all outcome-based secondary scoring before any sealed read. This is a safety fallback, not completed secondary analysis.
